How I think about products

A loop, not a launch

Every project below went through the same six steps. The order matters: I've found that most bad product decisions are actually good decisions made one step too early.

  1. 01

    Problem

    State the problem in the user's words, not the solution's. If it only makes sense as a feature request, it isn't a problem yet.

  2. 02

    Users

    Who specifically, and what are they doing instead today? The workaround tells you how much the pain is actually worth.

  3. 03

    Insights

    Validate with research and data before committing. Separate what I measured from what I assumed, and label the difference.

  4. 04

    Product

    Scope the smallest version that tests the riskiest assumption. Decide explicitly what the product will refuse to do.

  5. 05

    Metrics

    Pick one North Star that can go down, plus a guardrail. Vanity metrics are the ones that only move in one direction.

  6. 06

    Iteration

    Ship, measure against a baseline, and change the product — not the target — when the evidence disagrees with me.

Featured AI Product · Regulated finance · Retrieval & evaluation

FundBot — a mutual-fund assistant that refuses to guess

A facts-only FAQ assistant over four Mirae Asset schemes. Every answer is short, carries the official source URL and the date that source was last read, and the system declines when retrieval isn't confident.

Problem

In regulated finance, a chatbot that invents an expense ratio is worse than no chatbot at all. Retail investors ask the same factual questions — exit loads, SIP minimums, ELSS lock-in, riskometer levels — and a fluent wrong answer is indistinguishable from a right one.

Product decisions

  • The model never supplies a fact. Generation only rephrases a passage retrieval already selected, under a prompt that forbids adding numbers. If the API key is missing, the quota is spent or the request times out, FundBot returns the retrieved passage verbatim. Answers get less conversational; they never get less correct.
  • Lexical retrieval over embeddings. sentence-transformers pulls in roughly 2 GB of torch — it doesn't fit a free serverless tier and makes cold starts tens of seconds. For 47 short, keyword-dense financial documents, hybrid BM25F + TF-IDF measured better per byte. The constraint shaped the architecture, and the evaluation proved the trade was fine.
  • The confidence floor came from a sweep, not a guess. Cosine similarity alone was fooled by short off-topic questions sharing one rare word. Adding IDF-coverage scoring and sweeping the threshold gave MIN_CONFIDENCE = 0.06: the hardest off-topic question peaks at 0.052, so 0.06 answers 97.6% of real questions with zero false answers.
  • Scope is enforced, not requested. A fund-affinity pass suppresses the other three schemes, so "exit load of the midcap fund" can't be answered with the large cap figure.

Architecture

1

Guardrails

PII → scope → advice. A blocked question never reaches the knowledge base or the model.

2

Retrieval

Hybrid BM25F + TF-IDF over 47 curated documents, with field weighting, bigrams and fund-affinity re-ranking. IDF coverage decides whether we know the answer at all.

3

Generation

DeepSeek via OpenRouter rephrases the retrieved passage. Optional — every failure falls back to extractive output.

4

Answer

Response + source URL + the date that source was last read, so the user can verify the claim instead of trusting the model.

Voice of customer · Product discovery

AI Discovery Engine

Problem

Product teams have more feedback than anyone can read, and it's scattered across app stores, forums and issue trackers. Manual synthesis doesn't scale, and reading one source biases the conclusion.

What I built

A discovery tool that collects reviews and comments from six public sources — Google Play, YouTube, Steam, GitHub Issues, Hacker News and RSS — and turns them into an executive summary, ranked pain points, feature requests, sentiment and product opportunities. A natural-language layer answers questions like "what is the biggest complaint?" against the collected set.

Product thinking

The output is shaped as a discovery artefact, not a chat transcript — themes and ranked pain points map directly to what a PM does next. Six heterogeneous sources is a deliberate choice: store reviews skew to complaints, GitHub and HN skew to power users, so any single source distorts the read.

Outcome

Compresses multi-source feedback synthesis into a single pass with an exportable report. Demonstrated capability — not deployed with real product teams.

Consumer commerce · Conversational AI

AI Grocery Shopping Assistant

Problem

AI product search breaks commercially the moment it recommends something the store doesn't stock. One out-of-catalog suggestion costs the trust that made the user try conversational search in the first place.

What I built

An India-first AI grocery experience: natural-language search, cart-aware basket insights, recommendations and recipe suggestions, wired into a complete browse → cart → checkout → order flow with reviews and a wishlist.

Product thinking

AI is constrained to the live catalog rather than left to free-associate, and the app degrades gracefully — with no API key it runs in demo mode from seed data instead of erroring. North Star defined as query-to-cart conversion, measured against a keyword-search baseline. Localisation is not cosmetic: rupee pricing, Indian cities, phone validation and Indian grocery brands.

Outcome

Deployed and publicly usable. A companion implementation rebuilds the recommendation service on FastAPI + SQLAlchemy with a mocked-LLM Pytest suite and documented architecture.

Problem

Three teams were each maintaining a separate manual KPI report for the same production floor. The data existed; the delay in getting to it was the constraint. Root-cause investigations ran for days because nobody could rank defect drivers without pulling a fresh extract by hand.

Discovery

Ran discovery with 8+ line engineers and quality leads across the three teams, then scoped four dashboard modules sequenced by decision impact rather than request volume — the loudest request was not the one that unblocked the most decisions.

Built

  • Unified Tableau dashboards covering 15+ machines across 5 production lines, replacing the three parallel manual reports with self-serve access.
  • An automated Python ETL pipeline over Oracle SQL and HiveQL, so the dashboards refreshed without an analyst in the loop.
  • Four reliability KPIs — MTTR, MTBF, Technical Availability and Technical Loss — defined so engineers could rank defect drivers directly.

Outcome

Adopted by 3 teams within 3 weeks and cut recurring manual reporting by roughly 85% (about 10 hours a week, estimated). Root-cause investigation moved from days to hours, and the work contributed to an 18% reduction in unplanned downtime.

Takeaway

Adoption was the real metric, not dashboard count. Sequencing by decision impact is why three teams switched in three weeks instead of politely ignoring a fourth reporting tool.

Growth strategy · Research · Metrics

ChatGPT Voice Input — India growth strategy

Research

Surveyed 30+ active Indian mobile ChatGPT users and ran 5–6 in-depth interviews with urban professionals aged 25–34. Found a steep awareness-to-habit drop: 42% knew voice existed, 28% had tried it, 6% used it regularly.

Insight

The barrier wasn't capability, it was reversibility. 68% reported accent or language recognition failures, 61% privacy discomfort in shared spaces, and 57% wanted to review text before sending. One bad send ended the habit — so the interface, which looks like a search bar, was teaching the wrong mental model.

Solution

Chose contextual modes plus a rich empty state over a one-shot onboarding flow, because onboarding fires once and modes work every session. Voice becomes the primary CTA; a persistent "no auto-send · review first" badge answers the privacy anxiety; auto-corrections are visible and reversible. Explicitly ruled out auto-send and an always-on mic.

Metrics

North Star: weekly active voice conversation users, targeting 6% → 25% in three months. Guardrails on Hinglish transcription accuracy and voice CSAT, with a stated refusal to count raw mic taps as success.

Activation · Funnel teardown · PLG

n8n — onboarding & activation teardown

Problem

n8n's product is strong; its first ten minutes are not. New users complete a mandatory four-question survey, get redirected to a YouTube video, and then land on a completely blank canvas with no starting point.

Insight

In an estimated funnel built from PLG benchmarks and community data, the largest single drop — 27 points — sits between entering the dashboard and creating a first node. That's blank-canvas paralysis, and it's a design problem, not a user-intelligence problem. The survey itself is defensible; the gap is that nothing the user tells n8n changes what they see next.

Recommendation

P0: open to a pre-populated workflow that runs immediately with no credentials, and actually use the survey data to personalise the landing state. P1: replace the video with in-canvas guidance, and add a credential-free sandbox for day zero.

Metrics

North Star: time to first successful workflow execution — value delivered, not checklist completion. Proposed as A/B tests with day-7 retention as the guardrail.

EdTech · Monetisation · Localisation

Eureka by ALLEN — product teardown

Problem

Eureka's story-led, narrative-driven lessons are its strongest differentiator in Indian EdTech — but the funnel asks students to commit to a trial or enrolment before they experience that value.

Insight

Two frictions compound: a premature upsell and English-only content, which creates a comprehension wall for learners who don't think in English first. The pedagogy never gets a chance to do its job.

Recommendation

Let students experience the product in their own language before asking them to commit — a localisation-first fix to a monetisation-timing problem.

Format

A seven-slide visual teardown plus a one-page written summary covering product understanding, biggest strength, biggest weakness and a single recommendation.
